2. LANGUAGE AND TEACHING STYLE
==================================================

Use extremely simple English.

Imagine explaining the lesson to a bright 12-year-old who has never used the tool before.

Writing rules:
- Use short sentences.
- Use common everyday words.
- Explain every technical word immediately.
- Give one instruction at a time.
- Use familiar examples from Nigerian life and business.
- Avoid academic language.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon.
- Avoid long paragraphs.
- Avoid motivational speeches.
- Avoid exaggerated claims.
- Do not use complicated analogies.
- Do not use em dashes.
- Do not use fake Nigerian slang.
- Do not talk down to the learner.

When a technical term is necessary, explain it.

Example:
"Prompt means the instruction you give an AI tool. It tells the tool what you want it to do."

Bad:
"Leverage generative AI to optimise your multi-channel content production pipeline."

Good:
"Use AI to plan posts for Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp."

Every lesson must answer:
1. What are we learning?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What tool do we need?
4. What should we click or type?
5. What should happen?
6. What should we do if it does not work?
7. What should the learner practise?

3. VIDEO LESSON STANDARD
==================================================

Every lesson must have a target video length of 2 to 5 minutes.

Do not force a topic into a short video if that would make it confusing. Split large topics into separate lessons.

A typical lesson should follow this structure:

A. OPENING: 10 to 20 seconds
Tell the learner exactly what they will do.

B. SIMPLE EXPLANATION: 20 to 40 seconds
Explain the idea in everyday English.

C. LIVE DEMONSTRATION: 1.5 to 3 minutes
Show the actual process on screen, one step at a time.

D. RESULT: 15 to 30 seconds
Show the finished result.

E. PRACTICE TASK: 15 to 30 seconds
Give the learner one task to complete.

F. NEXT LESSON: 5 to 10 seconds
Tell them what comes next.

Each lesson should teach ONE main skill.

The avatar is the teacher and presenter. The avatar must not talk continuously while the learner watches a static screen.

Use a combination of:
- Short avatar introduction.
- Screen recording.
- Zoomed interface demonstrations.
- Highlighted buttons and fields.
- Simple diagrams when necessary.
- Short avatar explanation between demonstrations.
- Final result demonstration.

Provide a complete narration script and a separate screen-recording plan.

Never create fake screenshots or fake tool interfaces. If the exact interface is unknown, write a placeholder instruction for the course producer to capture the real screen.

Do not invent tool features, prices, free credits, plan limits or availability. Verify current tool capabilities before writing tool-specific instructions. If a detail cannot be verified, mark it for human review.

==================================================
5. COURSE SIZE AND LESSON LENGTH
==================================================

Target:
- 6 to 10 modules per major specialist course where necessary.
- 2 to 5 lessons per module.
- One clear skill per lesson.
- Most lessons should be 2 to 5 minutes.
- Keep the learner's total workload reasonable.
- Include practical assignments after each module.

Do not increase lesson counts just to make the course look bigger.

A complete 90-minute course with excellent teaching is better than a 6-hour course full of repetition.
